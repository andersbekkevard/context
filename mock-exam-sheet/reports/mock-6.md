## High-priority surfacing

- **Bias–variance cross-term justification** (D, A) — both cross-terms vanish: (i) $\varepsilon_0 \perp \hat f$ (training set independent of test noise); (ii) $\mathbb{E}[\hat f - \mathbb{E}\hat f] = 0$ by construction. Prof flagged "make explicit *why* each vanishes" — ISLP states result without spelling out independence assumption. (Q3a-i) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:269–317 — compact: `cross-terms vanish iff (a) train ⊥ test noise, (b) zero-mean centering`.
- **$\hat\mu_c = c\bar y$ optimal shrinkage**: $c^\star = \mu^2 / (\mu^2 + \sigma^2/n)$ (D) — derivation: $\text{Bias}^2 = (1-c)^2\mu^2$, $\text{Var} = c^2\sigma^2/n$, minimize → set derivative to zero. Limit: $\sigma^2/n \gg \mu^2 \Rightarrow c^\star \to 0$ (collapse to zero). Not in ISLP as worked example. (Q3a-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:320–339 — compact: `c* = μ²/(μ²+σ²/n)`.
- **"Trade-off" skepticism — two named regimes** (A, D) — prof skeptical of "trade-off" wording: (1) ridge on collinear OLS reduces *both* bias and variance vs OLS; (2) benign overfitting / double descent in $p \gg n$. Not framed this way in ISLP. (Q2a-ii, Q3a-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:79–85, 342–356 — compact: `ridge-vs-OLS-collinear; double descent — both can drop together`.
- **Wrong-way CV — screening before CV is biased *downward*** (B, C) — ISLP ex.5.3 exact pipeline: filter top-50 correlations across full data, then 10-fold CV; held-out folds informed the screen. Reports near-0% even on pure noise. Fix: screen *inside* each training fold. Prof emphasized direction explicitly. (Q2b-iii, Q3b-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:107–112, 395–401 — compact: `screen inside fold; otherwise downward-biased`.
- **LOOCV vs k-fold variance direction** (B) — LOOCV has *higher* variance than 5-fold (training sets overlap → fold errors strongly correlated → average doesn't reduce variance). Counter-intuitive direction; prof's L11 quote: "leave-one-out has better bias but K-fold has much better variance, and typically you're winning by having less variance." (Q2b-i, Q2b-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:96–106 — compact: `LOOCV: low bias, high variance; k-fold: opposite`.
- **1-SE rule — exact form** (C) — pick *largest* $\lambda$ (simplest model) such that $\text{CV}(\lambda) \le \text{CV}(\hat\lambda_{\min}) + \widehat{\text{SE}}$. Direction (which way along $\lambda$) is the trap. (Q2b-iv, Q4b-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:114–117, 547–550 — compact: `λ_1SE = max{λ: CV(λ) ≤ CV(λ_min) + SE}`.
- **Mini-batch SGD: noise = regularization, direction trap** (B) — *Larger* batch → *less* gradient noise → *less* implicit $L^2$ regularization. Mock exam reverses the direction as a trap. (Q2c-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:133–136 — compact: `small batch = noisier grad = stronger implicit reg`.
- **Continuous × categorical interaction — per-level slope** (B) — slope for level $c$ = (main effect of continuous) + (interaction with $c$); for reference level the interaction term is zero. Standard half-credit trap: report interaction term *alone*. Recurs three times in mock-6. (Q2d-iii, Q4a-iii, Q5a-i, Q5a-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:152–157, 477–490, 624–642 — compact: `slope|level = β_main + β_interact:level`.
- **Odds ↔ probability + $\Delta$-EUR multiplier** (B) — $p = \text{odds}/(1+\text{odds})$ (and inverse). For $\Delta$-unit change in $x$: odds multiplier $= \exp(\hat\beta \cdot \Delta)$, not $\exp(\hat\beta)^\Delta$ in writing (same value, framing). (Q2d-i, Q2d-ii, Q5a-i) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:145–149, 626–633 — compact: `p = odds/(1+odds); OR(Δx) = exp(βΔ)`.
- **Encoding-flip invariants (linear and logistic)** (B, C) — under change of reference level: RSE / residual deviance / fitted values / predicted probabilities all **unchanged**; individual coefficients and SEs **change** (different basis). Coefficient on a continuous predictor with interaction *also* changes because it now reports the slope at the new reference. (Q4a-iv, Q5a-v) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:493–508, 683–693 — compact: `re-encoding: fits invariant; coefficients change`.
- **$\text{Var}(\hat\beta) = \sigma^2 (X^\top X)^{-1}$ + collinearity intuition** (D, A) — near-collinearity → near-zero eigenvalue of $X^\top X$ → diagonal of inverse blows up → individual SEs explode while the *sum* / well-conditioned combination is stable. "Million minus million" intuition. ISLP discusses VIF but not the eigenvalue/inverse view explicitly. (Q4a-ii, Q2e-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:166–173, 451–470 — compact: `near-collinear: (X'X)⁻¹ inflates diag, off-diag negative cancel`.
- **CI vs PI formulae** (D) — CI: $\hat y_0 \pm t \hat\sigma \sqrt{x_0^\top(X^\top X)^{-1} x_0}$. PI: $\hat y_0 \pm t \hat\sigma \sqrt{1 + x_0^\top(X^\top X)^{-1} x_0}$. The $+1$ is the irreducible $\sigma^2$ for the new observation. As $n \to \infty$ CI shrinks to a point; PI is bounded below by $\pm t\hat\sigma$. (Q4a-v) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:510–522 — compact: `CI: √(x'(X'X)⁻¹x); PI: √(1 + same)`.
- **Bias–variance reading of $\text{OLS} > \lambda_{1\text{SE}} > \lambda_{\min}$ test-MSE** (B, C) — if ridge beats OLS, OLS is *over*-fitting (variance-dominated, often via collinearity); $\lambda_{1\text{SE}}$ sits between because it shrinks further → more bias, less variance saving. Direction reading is the test. (Q4b-iv) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:553–565 — compact: `OLS beats ridge ⇒ underfit; ridge beats OLS ⇒ overfit; 1-SE sits between`.
- **Linear-vs-boosting test-MSE gap diagnoses non-linearity / interactions** (B) — Ridge merely shrinks linear coefs; boosting (sum of small trees) captures non-linear shape + predictor interactions. Large drop from ridge to boosting ⇒ truth is non-linear and/or interacting. Useful diagnostic frame. (Q4c-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:605–611 — compact: `ridge→boost big drop ⇒ non-linearity/interactions present`.
- **$M$–$\nu$ coupling in gradient boosting** (C) — halving $\nu$ ≈ doubling required $M$; $M \cdot \nu \approx$ const for given capacity. $\nu$ fixed small ($\le 0.1$); $M$ tuned by CV/early stopping; $d$ tuned by CV. ISLP mentions but doesn't emphasize this coupling. (Q4c-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:590–602 — compact: `M·ν ≈ const; halve ν ⇒ ~2× M`.
- **AdaBoost $\alpha_m$ + weight-multiplier numerics** (D) — $\alpha_m = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{1-\text{err}_m}{\text{err}_m}$. Misclassified weight multiplied by $\exp(\alpha_m)$, correctly classified by $\exp(-\alpha_m)$. Prof has used both factor-of-½ and unscaled conventions; either is fine if consistent. For err=0.3: α≈0.42, miss×1.53, correct×0.65. (Q5b-ii, Q5b-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:733–753 — compact: `α = ½log((1-err)/err); miss×e^α, correct×e^(-α)`.
- **"Why weak learners" — bias–variance for boosting** (B, D) — boosting builds high-bias-low-variance ensemble out of many simple learners; deep base learners would inherit per-tree variance and overfit quickly. Stumps: high bias individually, low variance; sequential corrections drive bias down without re-introducing variance. (Q5b-iv) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:756–763 — compact: `deep base ⇒ ensemble inherits high var; stumps balance`.
- **NN parameter count with biases** (D) — per-layer $(\text{in} + 1) \cdot \text{out}$. Mock: $(7+1) \cdot 16 + (16+1) \cdot 1 = 128 + 17 = 145$. Dropout/early stopping/label smoothing add **zero** parameters. The trap: forgetting biases (gives 144 or 128). (Q5c-i) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:766–777 — compact: `params = Σ (in+1)·out per layer; reg techniques add 0`.
- **Dropout is training-only; 20% rule** (B) — at inference dropout is *off* (no random zeroing). 20% is "very common"; 50% is "too aggressive, never use" (prof L24). ISLP's framing differs slightly. (Q2h-i, Q2h-ii, Q5c-v) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:225–230, 810–813 — compact: `dropout: train-only; 20% common, 50% too aggressive`.
- **Prof: "always regularize" NN** (A) — even small architectures relative to $n$ should have *some* regularizer; mini-batch SGD itself counts. Prof L26: "I can't think of an example where you'd ever want to train a neural network without regularization." (Q2h-iv) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:232–235 — compact: `NN: always some regularization (SGD noise counts)`.
- **Deep linear net = linear regression** (D) — without non-linearity, composition $W_L \cdots W_1$ collapses to one matrix $W_{\text{eff}}$; no representational gain from depth. (Q5c-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:787–791 — compact: `identity activations ⇒ depth = linear regression`.
- **LDA assumptions + small-class bias–variance argument** (B, D) — assumptions: (1) class-conditional multivariate normal; (2) common pooled $\Sigma$ across classes. QDA fits $\Sigma_k$ per class → $p(p+1)/2$ params per class → small-class $\hat\Sigma_k$ noisy. LDA's pooled estimate has lower variance; bias hit small if true covariances similar. (Q5d-i) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:823–836 — compact: `LDA: pooled Σ (lower var); QDA: per-class Σ (high var when class small)`.
- **mtry = √p classification default + decorrelation** (B, C) — classification: $\mathtt{mtry} = \lceil\sqrt p\rceil$; regression: $p/3$. Reason for $\mathtt{mtry} < p$: forces trees to use different top-split predictors → decorrelates trees → variance reduction from averaging is bigger. Bagging ($\mathtt{mtry} = p$) gives highly correlated trees. (Q5d-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:838–843 — compact: `clf: √p; reg: p/3; <p ⇒ decorrelate ⇒ var↓`.
- **Variable importance: what it does NOT tell you** (B) — VI does NOT give *direction* (sign), *shape* (linear? saturating?), or *causality*. Use PDPs / ICE / parametric models to recover these. Recurring exam pattern. (Q5d-iii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:845–852 — compact: `VI ≠ direction, shape, causality (need PDP/ICE)`.
- **Baseline / null classifier under imbalance** (B, C) — trivial constant classifier achieves error = (minority class rate). Here 26%. Always-no-churn beats nothing; accuracy vs baseline is the right comparison. Prefer AUC, (sensitivity, specificity), or cost-weighted metric in imbalanced settings. (Q5e-i, Q5e-ii) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:856–870 — compact: `null err = minority rate; use AUC/sens-spec under imbalance`.
- **Threshold 0.5 vs base rate** (B) — default 0.5 inappropriate when base rate is far from 0.5. With base rate 26%, $\hat p = 0.35$ is well above base rate but below 0.5 → classified "no churn" by default rule, but model is signalling elevated risk. A threshold ≈ base rate is more sensible. (Q5a-iv) — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:676–680 — compact: `threshold ≈ base rate when imbalanced; 0.5 is misleading default`.

## Lower-priority but useful

- **Bootstrap SE formula** (D) — $\widehat{\text{SE}}_{\text{boot}} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{B-1}\sum_b (\hat\theta_b^* - \bar\theta^*)^2}$. With replacement; centered on $\hat\theta$ not $\theta$ (so bias not corrected); typical $B \in [1000, 10000]$. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:181–198 — compact: `SE_boot = sample SD of θ*; with replacement; centered on θ̂`.
- **$K$-dummy + intercept rank deficiency** (B) — $K$ dummies + intercept is rank-deficient (dummies sum to 1 = intercept column). Use $K-1$ dummies. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:174–177 — compact: `K-1 dummies + intercept (else singular)`.
- **Backprop in tiny net — exact chain** (D) — $\partial L / \partial w_2 = (\hat y - y) \cdot h$; $\partial L / \partial w_1 = (\hat y - y) \cdot w_2 \cdot h(1-h) \cdot x$. Sigmoid derivative: $\sigma'(z) = h(1-h)$. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:405–421 — compact: `∂L/∂w₁ = (ŷ-y)·w₂·h(1-h)·x`.
- **PCA: standardize, unit-norm loadings, *largest* variance** (B) — without standardization PC1 dominated by largest-variance variable; loadings $\|\phi_j\|_2 = 1$ by construction; PC1 maximizes (not minimizes) variance. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:240–254 — compact: `PCA: standardize; ||φ||=1; PC1 = max variance`.
- **Heteroscedasticity remedy** (C) — fanning residuals violates constant variance; remedies: transform response ($\log$, $\sqrt{\cdot}$) or weighted least squares. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:524–528 — compact: `fanning ⇒ heteroscedasticity ⇒ log/√ or WLS`.
- **Naive min-CV is downward-biased** (B, C) — taking argmin of noisy CV-MSE values and reporting that minimum is biased downward by selection. Honest pipeline requires outer loop. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:386–392 — compact: `report min-CV ⇒ selection bias ⇒ need outer loop`.
- **Gradient boosting one iteration** (C) — fit new tree to current residuals (= negative gradient under squared loss); update $\hat f \leftarrow \hat f + \nu \hat g$. Initialize at $\bar y$. — mock-exam-6-solution.tex:569–584 — compact: `fit tree to r_i, update f̂ ← f̂ + ν·ĝ`.
