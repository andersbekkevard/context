## High-priority surfacing

- **Implicit L2 regularization of mini-batch SGD** (A, D) — Prof's "super weird" headline: over-parameterized regime, mini-batch SGD selects (approximately) the min-L2-norm interpolant among the many zero-loss solutions — not training-loss minimum (every interpolating solution already has zero training loss). — Q1(5), Q3b(ii). — `mock-exam-9.tex:59`, solution `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:369`. — One-liner: "SGD → implicit min-‖θ‖₂ among interpolators."
- **Dropout default p ≈ 0.2, not 0.5** (B, C) — Prof's recommendation; mock 9 explicitly flags p=0.5 as the wrong default. ISLP and folklore often quote 0.5 — calibrate to prof. — Q2e(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:154`. — One-liner: "Default p ≈ 0.2 (prof); 0.5 is NOT recommended."
- **Backprop ≠ parameter update + DAG only (no plain RNN)** (A, B) — Backprop is gradient-computation (chain rule + stored intermediates), separate from SGD update; vanilla backprop needs DAG, RNN requires BPTT. — Q2c(i)(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:121,128`. — One-liner: "Backprop = chain-rule grad; update is SGD. DAG only; RNN→BPTT."
- **Permutation importance > impurity/Gini-based** (B) — Prof's stated preference for permutation-based VI ("makes more sense"). Common trap: Gini/impurity. — Q1(6). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:49,60`. — One-liner: "Prof prefers permutation VI over Gini-impurity."
- **OOB ≈ 1/3 (not 2/3); 2/3 is in-bag** (B, D) — Asymptotic (1-1/n)^n → 1/e ≈ 0.368; numeric: n·1/e ≈ 368 for n=1000. n=5: (4/5)^5 = 1024/3125 = 0.328. — Q1(7), Q2i(iii), Q3c. — `mock-exam-9.tex:61`, `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:240,386`. — One-liner: "OOB ≈ 1/e ≈ 36.8% (n=5: 0.328). In-bag ≈ 63.2%."
- **B in RF is NOT a CV-tuned hyperparameter; M in boosting IS** (C) — Crucial contrast: averaging only reduces variance, so RF test error is monotone non-increasing in B (just pick "enough" ≈ 500–1000). Boosting can overfit → M must be CV-tuned. — Q2i(ii), Q4d(iv). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:233,529`. — One-liner: "RF: more B = harmless. Boost: more M = overfit. Use CV-curve to pick M."
- **LOOCV bias-variance reversal** (B) — LOOCV has LOWER bias (trains on n-1) but HIGHER variance than k-fold because n fold errors are nearly perfectly correlated (overlapping training sets). "Average of more terms" intuition fails under correlation. — Q2b(i)(ii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:97,101`. — One-liner: "LOOCV: low bias, HIGH variance (correlated folds)."
- **XGBoost regularizer: Ω(T) = γ|T| + ½λ‖w‖² + α‖w‖₁** (A) — Three explicit knobs beyond vanilla GBM. λ ~ ridge on leaves; α ~ lasso on leaves (drives small leaves exactly to 0); γ|T| ~ cost-complexity pruning (split rejected unless gain > γ). ALSO: η (shrinkage) still exists. — Q1(10), Q2f, Q5c. — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:176,665`. — One-liner: "Ω = γ|T| + ½λ‖w‖² + α‖w‖₁. Keep η."
- **XGBoost uses gradient + Hessian (2nd-order Newton step)** (A) — g_i = ∂L/∂ŷ_i AND h_i = ∂²L/∂ŷ_i². Vanilla GBM uses only g_i. Newton step ≈ scales by local curvature. — Q1(10), Q2f(i), Q5c(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:171,656`. — One-liner: "XGBoost: 1st AND 2nd derivative; GBM: 1st only."
- **η·M coupling in boosting; doubling both ≠ "preserve balance"** (C) — Cumulative ensemble strength ≈ η·M. Doubling BOTH more-than-doubles effective correction → variance/overfit. Pick M from CV-error curve at fixed η. — Q5c(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:684`. — One-liner: "η·M ≈ ensemble strength. Tune M via CV at fixed η."
- **1-SE rule mechanics (table)** (C) — bound = CV* + SE*; among candidates ≤ bound, pick the SIMPLEST (here largest λ, smallest M). Mock works the table explicitly twice (ridge; PCR). — Q2b(iii), Q4c(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:108,476`. — One-liner: "bound = CV_min + SE_min; pick simplest below bound."
- **PCR-vs-ridge: compression vs shrinkage** (C) — Prefer PCR when you believe in low-rank latent structure (compression); prefer ridge for unit-preserving shrinkage on original predictors. Training-MSE picks M=4 (full PCR = OLS rotation), so CV is essential. — Q4b(iii), Q4c(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:468,499`. — One-liner: "PCR = compression (latent structure). Ridge = shrinkage (keep units)."
- **Dummy-variable trap: K levels → K-1 dummies + intercept** (A) — Using K dummies + intercept makes 4 columns sum to all-ones → singular X'X. Q4 reference level is American. — Q4a(ii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:418`. — One-liner: "K levels → K-1 dummies + intercept (else singular)."
- **Joint F-test for B-spline block** (C, D) — "Weight has no effect" = β₁=β₂=β₃=β₄=0 simultaneously → joint partial-F test on the 4 spline coefficients, NOT 4 separate t-tests. — Q4a(iv). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:434`. — One-liner: "Joint F for the whole spline block."
- **Collinearity fingerprint in OLS printout** (B) — Two symptoms together: (1) large SE / small |t| / huge p on one of the correlated predictors despite obvious relationship; (2) joint/overall F highly significant. Mechanism: σ²(X'X)⁻¹ inflates in near-singular direction. Drop one → near-identical prediction (collinearity hurts INTERPRETATION, not PREDICTION). — Q2g(ii), Q4b(i)(ii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:194,451`. — One-liner: "Individually insig + jointly sig + drop-one ≈ same MSE ⇒ collinearity."
- **edf → λ direction** (B) — Smaller edf ⇔ LARGER λ (more penalty). edf=1.0 ⇒ smoother collapses to a straight line in that predictor. — Q4d(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:510`. — One-liner: "Smaller edf ⇔ larger λ; edf=1 ⇒ linear fit."
- **Logistic interaction: sign of marginal effect depends on the other variable** (B) — With β_stu + β_bal:stu · bal in linear predictor, sign of student effect on default flips with balance level. ISLP §4.3.5 makes this point on Default. — Q2h. — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:218`. — One-liner: "Sign of effect = sign of (β_main + β_int·x_other)."
- **Odds-multiplier ≠ probability-multiplier** (B) — exp(β) multiplies odds; p_new = (OR·p)/(1 - (1-OR)·p), NOT OR·p. Only approx equal for small p. Mock 9 explicitly computes the gap: owner 0.345 vs naive 0.230 at p=0.562. — Q5a(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:566`. — One-liner: "p_new = OR·p / (1 - (1-OR)·p). OR scales odds, not p."
- **LDA discriminant: which terms cancel and why** (D) — log(π_k f_k(x)) → δ_k requires dropping (i) constants in p, (ii) -½ log|Σ|, AND (iii) the QUADRATIC -½x'Σ⁻¹x because Σ is shared. The last is what makes the boundary linear; under QDA it survives as -½x'(Σ_A⁻¹ - Σ_B⁻¹)x → conic. — Q3a(i)(iii), Q5b(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:267,295,638`. — One-liner: "LDA drops shared -½x'Σ⁻¹x. QDA keeps it ⇒ quadratic boundary."
- **LDA boundary with priors: c shifts by log(π_A/π_B)** (D) — Equal priors: x₁+x₂=3 (perpendicular bisector midpoint). With π_A=0.8, π_B=0.2: c → 3 + ½ log(0.8/0.2) = 3 + ½ log 4 ≈ 3.693. Boundary moves AWAY from the higher-prior mean's region (its region grows). — Q3a(ii)(iv), Q5b(iv). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:284,311,647`. — One-liner: "Equal-Σ LDA boundary: perp bisector + (½‖proj‖) log(π_A/π_B) shift away from majority."
- **Mini-batch SGD: unbiased grad, count = ⌊N/m⌋ per epoch** (A, B) — E[∇̂L] = full-batch gradient; variance ∝ 1/m. Powers-of-2 batch sizes are HARDWARE (GPU/SIMD), NOT statistics. 200,000/256 = 781 updates/epoch (782 with partial batch). — Q2d. — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:137,141,145`. — One-liner: "Unbiased, var ∝ 1/m. pow-2 = hardware. updates/epoch = ⌊N/m⌋."
- **RF mtry controls inter-tree correlation ρ** (A) — V(T̄) = ρσ² + (1-ρ)σ²/B. Smaller mtry → smaller ρ → more variance reduction from averaging. — Q2i(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:230`. — One-liner: "V(T̄) = ρσ² + (1-ρ)σ²/B; small mtry → small ρ."
- **Standardization does NOT fix collinearity** (B) — Standardization rescales columns; doesn't change angle between them; rank(X'X) unchanged. Only matters for shrinkage/unit-free interpretation. — Q2g(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:188`. — One-liner: "Standardize ≠ orthogonalize. Rank unchanged."
- **Adjusted R² can DECREASE on added predictors; R² cannot** (B) — R²_adj = 1 - (1-R²)(n-1)/(n-p-1); gains in R² may not overcome the p penalty. — Q2g(iii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:199`. — One-liner: "R²_adj formula; can drop when R² gain < p-penalty."
- **Trivial baseline accuracy on imbalanced data** (B) — Always-predict-majority accuracy = 1200/1500 = 0.80, only 4–5 pp below the 0.84–0.85 of three classifiers. Accuracy is dominated by majority class; use sens/spec/AUC. — Q5d(i). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:704`. — One-liner: "Baseline acc = majority frac. Use sens/spec/AUC under imbalance."
- **ROC threshold trade-off: lower τ → higher sens, lower spec** (B, C) — Moving the threshold down classifies more as positive; sensitivity ↑, specificity ↓. Pick τ at the spec-floor for max sens. — Q5d(ii). — `mock-exam-9-solution.tex:719`. — One-liner: "Lower threshold: sens↑, spec↓."
