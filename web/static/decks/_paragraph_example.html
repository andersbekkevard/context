Question 1 4 points

In supervised statistical learning, which goal is most associated with caring about the actual values of the model parameters rather than the model's accuracy on new data?

  • A Prediction
  • B Inference
  • C Variance reduction
  • D Unsupervised learning
Show answer
Correct answer: B

Inference is the goal where we care about how the inputs relate to the output: which variables matter, with what sign, with what magnitude. Prediction (A) only cares whether $\hat f(x)$ is close to $y$ on new data — the parameters are means to an end and need not be interpretable.

C is a property of estimators, not a goal. D is a different paradigm entirely (no response variable to predict or explain).

Atoms: prediction-vs-inference.

  1. parametric — inference needs a model whose coefficients we can interpret; non-parametric methods don't expose interpretable parameters.
  2. understand — inference is about understanding the relationship, not about minimising any loss; "predict" / "minimise prediction error" describe the prediction goal instead.
  3. non-parametric — these are the flexible/complex models that pay for flexibility with reduced interpretability. "Regression" and "classification" are problem types, not flexibility classes.
  4. less interpretable — direct consequence of higher complexity.
  5. model selection bias — Lasso embeds selection in the same fit as estimation, so the resulting coefficients aren't the post-selection biased estimates you get from running OLS on a subset chosen by AIC. (The prof's explicit framing in lecture.)
  6. tree pruning — the cost-complexity penalty $\alpha |T|$ is the tree analogue of the $\lambda$-shrinkage penalty. Bagging/RF/boosting are ensemble methods, not shrinkage, and are common distractors.
  7. weight penalisation — the L1/L2 penalty on weights is the direct shrinkage analogue. Dropout and early stopping also fight over-fitting but are not shrinkage; this is a deliberate trap.
  8. reduce test error — the whole point of regularisation is to trade a little training-set fit for better generalisation. "Reduce training error" is the opposite. "Shrink parameters" is the mechanism, not the goal.
  9. response variable — defining feature of unsupervised learning: no $Y$ to predict or explain.
  10. groups — both PCA (directions of variation) and clustering (cluster assignments) surface group structure in the data.
