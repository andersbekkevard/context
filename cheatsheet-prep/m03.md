# Module 03 — Linear regression — cheat-sheet candidates

## MUST

- **Interaction trap (sex × age etc.).** With $y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X + \beta_2 Z + \beta_3 X Z$, the "main effect" $\beta_2$ is the $Z$-effect **only at $X=0$**. Group equations: $Z=0 \Rightarrow \hat y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X$; $Z=1 \Rightarrow \hat y = (\beta_0+\beta_2) + (\beta_1+\beta_3) X$. Quoting "males weigh 5kg more than females" from a `sex` coef when `sex×age` is in the model = WRONG unless age=0. **State your coding** ("assuming male=0, female=1").

- **K-level factor encoding.** Use $K-1$ dummies + reference level (R picks alphabetical first). Coefficients are **differences from reference**. NEVER code 0/1/2 (imposes order). Including all $K$ → $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}$ singular. **Sign flips** if reference swaps. Df consumed = $K-1$.

- **Main-effects rule (hierarchical principle).** Including $X\cdot Z$ requires $X$ and $Z$ as separate terms. `Y ~ X:Z` alone = wrong. Non-negotiable.

- **Statistical vs practical significance.** Stat sig = is effect real (small $p$, narrow CI). Practical sig = is slope big enough to matter. $\mathrm{SE}(\hat\beta_j) \propto 1/\sqrt{n}$ so any nonzero effect becomes significant for big $n$. **"Significance is just sample size."** Always quote effect size with $p$.

- **What $p$ is NOT (CE1 2g trap).** $p = P(\text{data as extreme} \mid H_0)$. FALSE: "$1-p$ = prob $H_0$ true"; "$p>0.05 \Rightarrow H_1$ false"; "$p$ = prob results happened by chance"; "fail to reject = null true."

- **Independence is the load-bearing assumption.** 5 assumptions: (1) Gaussian (2) zero mean (3) homoscedastic (4) indep of other vars (5) indep of each other. **Violating (4)/(5) "ruins everything"**: point estimates still unbiased, but SE/CI/p all wrong. Time-series, spatial, repeated measures = silent killer. Gaussian/zero-mean/homoscedastic are robust.

- **Heteroscedasticity → SE unreliable.** Spot: residuals-vs-fitted fanning, or scale-location plot trending. Fit still unbiased, but reported CIs/p-values are wrong. Fix: transform $Y$ (often $\log Y$), weighted LS, or GLM.

- **Diagnostic plot reading (the four autoplot panels).**
  - **Residuals vs fitted**: want no structure. Curvature → linearity broken. Fanning → heteroscedasticity. Clusters → independence broken.
  - **QQ plot** (prof: "kind of thing I would put on a test"): theoretical quantiles (x) vs standardized residuals (y). Straight line ⇒ Gaussian. S-shape ⇒ heavy/light tails. Banana ⇒ skew. Endpoint kinks ⇒ outliers. Tail wobble is normal even under truth.
  - **Scale-location**: $\sqrt{|\tilde r_i|}$ vs fitted. Flat line ⇒ homoscedastic. Trend ⇒ variance scales with mean.
  - **Residuals vs leverage**: **dangerous corner = top-right or bottom-right** (high $h_{ii}$ AND large $|\tilde r|$). Cook's distance contours flag influence.

- **Leverage vs influence (fat-kid-on-seesaw).** Leverage $h_{ii} \in [1/n, 1]$ is property of $\mathbf{X}$ alone (no $\mathbf{y}$). High leverage = extreme $x$. **Leverage ≠ outlier.** Influence = high leverage AND large residual. Simple-LR: $h_{ii} = \frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x_i-\bar x)^2}{\sum_j (x_j - \bar x)^2}$. Multi: $h_{ii} = \mathbf{x}_i^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_i$. Trace: $\sum h_{ii} = p+1$, average $=(p+1)/n$.

- **Cook's distance.** $D_i \propto \tilde r_i^2 \cdot h_{ii}/(1-h_{ii})$. Examine if $>0.5$, definitely if $>1$.

- **Standardized vs raw residuals.** Raw: $\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{e}) = \sigma^2(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H})$, unequal variance + correlated. **Standardized**: $\tilde r_i = e_i/(\hat\sigma\sqrt{1-h_{ii}})$ → ~unit variance. Studentized: swap $\hat\sigma_{(i)}$. $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ unchanged — diagnostics only.

- **Collinearity symptoms (output diagnosis).** Two correlated predictors → high SEs, individual $t$'s insignificant BUT joint $F$ highly significant. Signs/magnitudes flip across resamples. Predictions OK (sum $\beta_1 x_1+\beta_2 x_2$ stable) but **interpretation broken**. Algebra: $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}$ near-singular → $(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}$ blows up → $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta_j)\to\infty$. Fixes: drop, combine, ridge, PCR. Perfect collinearity if $p>n$ or $K$ dummies for $K$ levels.

- **"Only individual p-values is dangerous."** Sequence: F-test first ("any predictor useful?"), then drill into t's. Collinearity hides signal in individual t's.

- **CI vs PI distinction.**
  - CI for mean response: $\mathbf{x}_0^\top\hat{\boldsymbol\beta} \pm t_{\alpha/2,\,n-p-1}\hat\sigma\sqrt{\mathbf{x}_0^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_0}$.
  - PI for future obs: same $\pm t\cdot\hat\sigma\sqrt{1 + \mathbf{x}_0^\top(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{x}_0}$.
  - **+1 under sqrt = irreducible noise → PI ALWAYS wider than CI.**
  - Both narrowest at centroid, fan out at extremes.
  - **Frequentist interpretation**: across many resamples, ~95% of intervals cover true $\beta$. Once computed, $\beta$ is in or not. "95% prob this specific interval covers $\beta$" = WRONG.

- **"Linear" = linear in parameters, not in X.** $y=\beta_0+\beta_1 x+\beta_2 x^2$ is linear regression. Same OLS machinery for $\log X$, $\sqrt X$, $\sin X$, polynomial.

- **Errors vs residuals.** $\varepsilon_i$ = unobservable random variable, can only be **predicted**, not estimated. $e_i = y_i - \hat y_i$ = prediction of $\varepsilon_i$.

- **"Fancy correlations, not causal."** Regression ≠ causation. A significant slope can be a lurking variable; intelligence ↔ wealth example.

- **Reading `lm()` output table.** Per coefficient: Estimate, Std. Error, t value, Pr(>|t|). Quick CI: $\hat\beta_j \pm 2\cdot\mathrm{SE}$. Significant at 5% iff $|t|\gtrsim 2$.

## NICE

- **Sampling distribution.** $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\sim N_{p+1}(\boldsymbol\beta,\,\sigma^2(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1})$. $\mathrm{Var}(\hat\beta_j)=\sigma^2 c_{jj}$, $c_{jj}=[(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}]_{jj}$. Unbiased.

- **t-test boxed.** $t_j = \hat\beta_j/(\hat\sigma\sqrt{c_{jj}}) \sim t_{n-p-1}$ under $H_0:\beta_j=0$. Simple-LR df = $n-2$.

- **Residual SE.** $\hat\sigma^2 = \mathrm{RSS}/(n-p-1)$ unbiased; $p+1$ df eaten by $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$. MLE divides by $n$ (biased; barely matters at large $n$).

- **Simple-LR SE intuition.** $\mathrm{SE}(\hat\beta_1)^2 = \sigma^2/\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2$. Reduce uncertainty by (i) larger $n$, (ii) wider spread of $x$.

- **Hat matrix properties.** $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{X}(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top$. Symmetric, idempotent ($\mathbf{H}^2=\mathbf{H}$), $\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{H})=p+1$. $\hat{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}$, $\mathbf{e}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H})\mathbf{y}$. $\hat{\mathbf{y}}\perp\mathbf{e}$. Depends only on $\mathbf{X}$.

- **Normal equations.** $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}\Rightarrow\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}$.

- **MLE ⇔ LS.** Under $\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2)$, max log-likelihood ⇔ min RSS (drop constants, factor out $1/(2\sigma^2)$). Other losses: $|y-\hat y|\Rightarrow$ Laplace errors, more robust.

- **R² caveats.** $R^2=1-\mathrm{RSS}/\mathrm{TSS}$; **never decreases** when adding params. Adjusted: $R^2_{\mathrm{adj}}=1-\frac{\mathrm{RSS}/(n-p-1)}{\mathrm{TSS}/(n-1)}$. Prof prefers test error.

- **LOOCV shortcut (OLS only).** $\mathrm{CV}_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum (y_i-\hat y_i)^2/(1-h_{ii})^2$. Works because $h_{ii}$ doesn't depend on $\mathbf{y}$.

- **$F = t^2$ identity.** $F_{1,n-p-1}=t^2_{n-p-1}$. Single-coef F equals squared t.

- **F-test concept only.** $H_0:\beta_1=\cdots=\beta_p=0$. Why use it: collinearity can hide signal in t's. **Prof won't ask mechanics.**

- **Remedies for assumption violations.** Curvature → transform X / polynomial / GAM. Fanning → transform Y / WLS / GLM. Non-Gaussian → transform Y / GLM. Correlated → mixed-effects / GLS / chunk-CV. Outlier → **investigate, don't delete**.

- **High dim ($p>n$).** $\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}$ necessarily singular → OLS no unique solution → need regularization (module 6).

- **"Bias" = intercept.** Prof prefers "bias" for $\beta_0$.
